Blossom

Off the keyboard

Six things, in order of how often

All of them are the same trick underneath: repeat a movement until your hands stop needing your attention. That's also, more or less, how I learned to code. Move the pointer and the petals scatter.

The six

Doubles, mostly, on whatever court is free. Everyone wants the smash; the shot that actually wins rallies is the net kill, which takes about a year to stop hitting into the tape.

A game about where the cue ball stops, not where the object ball goes — everyone learns that in the wrong order. Eight-ball, and enough nine-ball to be dangerous once a night.

Card flourishing with the magic taken out — no tricks, no misdirection, just a deck moving in ways it shouldn't. A move takes weeks to get clean and a minute to explain, which is roughly the inverse of writing code.

A spinning top on a string between two sticks. Spin it fast enough and it behaves like a gyroscope, which is the only reason the throws work at all. When cardistry fails, a card lands on the carpet. When this fails, it goes under the sofa.

Forwards confidently, backwards a lot less so. Skateboarding is in here too, still at the stage where the board goes further than I do.

Two strikes and then a gutter, every single time. I've stopped trying to work out why and started enjoying it.

The point

Why any of this is on a CV site

Because it's the same skill. A cardistry move takes six weeks of failed repetitions before it looks effortless, and nobody watching the finished version can tell. Debugging is that, with fewer dropped objects — and I'd rather show you the habit than claim it.
